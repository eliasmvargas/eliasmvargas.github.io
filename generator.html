DDS Function Generator

IMSAI Guy, one of my favorite electronics Youtubers, recommended those interested in tinkering with electronics to build their own equipment if their lab is lacking it. I've invested a fair amount into a nice oscilloscope (RIGOL DHO812), but I've been using a spliced 3.5mm audio cable and a pure tone generator on my computer as a makeshift function generator. As you might be able to guess, this has been far from ideal. I decided making a function generator would be a great solution!

Plan

I wanted something fairly compact and simple to use and reliable within the audible range. After some thinking, I came up with these requirements:

  • Powered by 12V DC wall wort
  • Reliable between 0-100kHz
  • 10V peak to peak max output
  • Sine, square, and triangle wave output
  • LCD display to show frequency and amplitude

At first, I thought of doing a purely analog function generator for the cool-ness factor of doing something without having to program it.. But I shortly realized that the analog options available were going to be significantly more complicated than it was worth, in my case. After some research I found the AD9833 direct digital synthesis chip, which is low power, easy to program, affordable, and had most of the output capability I was looking for. The only thing lacking was the adjustable output voltage. But this actually made for a great excuse to add some analog components into this project! The AD9833 actually has a much wider frequency range than I need, with a nominal max frequency of 12.5MHz, but signal quality begins to significantly degrade after around 8MHz. Fine by me! More than enough.
